    Why this direction looks this way

    B / Postmodernist exploration

    Original web composition informed by a viewed design reference. No museum artwork is reproduced. Audience effects below are intentions, not research findings.

    01 / Angular, structured overlap

    Ettore Sottsass, Carlton Room Divider, 1981 · The Met. Colored horizontal and diagonal members and stacked compartments become overlapping menu-like panels, an angled product frame, and a triangular tab. They aim to suggest a casual café noticeboard; the risk is a busier first impression.

    02 / More than added color

    The reference’s tension between a textured gray plinth and colored members becomes a fine patterned base beneath flat paper shapes. Serif invitation, plain sans-serif controls, and a monospace ticket mix visual languages. The intended effect is a familiar, collected quality; the risk is that the mix feels less coherent.

    03 / An open invitation

    The overlapping pieces suggest different people sharing a table, while the headline and size controls remain level and readable. This aims to welcome friends and a solo café visitor alike. The risk is that the collage signals a scene or club more strongly than the Everyperson brief intends.
