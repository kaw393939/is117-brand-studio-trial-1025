00 / Direction

Good company.
Everyday company.

Common Hour makes an ordinary white T-shirt feel at home in ordinary moments.

Design draft · Checks and simulated learner browser review pending

Primary situation

Coffee. Study. Friends.

A student wears the tee for café study sessions and time with friends. They want a recognizable brand without a loud logo.

Brand character

Everyperson

An approachable, familiar character. Warm, plainspoken, and unhurried; an archetype for the brand, not a label for every buyer.

Chosen direction

Gathered pieces

B’s serif invitation and layered green/apricot frame felt more like a café to the simulated learner. The storefront moves the size choice and action higher.

Audience hypotheses: the secondary persona is a solo café visitor joining friends who wants to feel welcome without a club atmosphere. Both cards are assumptions; no customer interviews or surveys have been conducted. View the two annotated hero studies.

05 / Interaction

One clear action.

The primary action adds a chosen size to a temporary demo bag. It does not place an order.

Add to demo bag ↗Try the size selector ↗

The green sample uses the storefront’s real button classes. Native size radios have visible focus and selected states. Missing-size feedback explains what to do; successful additions are announced in the demo bag. The bag clears on reload and has no checkout or persistence.

06 / Voice

Warm.
Plainspoken.
Unhurried.

An open invitation to a familiar situation, with no insider vocabulary or pressure.

Warm / A shared situation

“Good company. No occasion needed.”

A recognizably everyday invitation. It does not claim that buying the shirt creates friends or grants access to a group.

Plainspoken / A useful next step

“Choose a size.”

Simple interface language. The supporting line names coffee plans, study breaks, and time with people; the visitor decides whether that meaning fits.

07 / Ethical persuasion

Useful first.
Familiar by design.

Two Cialdini applications chosen by the simulated learner. These are intended mechanisms, not proven conversion improvements.

Reciprocity / Three free outfit ideas

The apricot “A few ideas, on us” section offers three short combinations using clothes a visitor may already have. It is visible without payment, signup, email, or a download. The benefit comes before any purchase decision.

Liking / Recognizable everyday moments

The headline and support use friendly café, study-break, and friends language tied to the audience hypotheses. The intended connection comes from familiar interests, without invented customer stories, quotes, group membership, or popularity claims.

The shirt, $28 fictional demo price, and S/M/L/XL sizes remain stable. No fabric, fit, delivery, sustainability, scarcity, review, or sales claim has been added.

A live reference. Color and space values come from the same CSS variables as the shop. Headlines, body copy, actions, and logo assets are shared. Compare this board with the real storefront when revising the identity.
